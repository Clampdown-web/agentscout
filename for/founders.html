AI agents for founders and small businesses (2026)

Ready-made agents for operations, support, marketing and building your first app, so a small team can cover more ground. We picked the top tools for 6 jobs founders and small businesses do every day: 36 tools in all, 27 of them free or with a free tier.

What this is

This page is for founders and small teams who need coverage, not a research stack. The jobs are business operations, workflow automation, building an app without code, customer support, marketing, and a personal assistant. It shows 36 tools from 1178 listings that match those tasks, across 6 sections: Business operations (6 of 213); Automate workflows (6 of 511); Build apps without code (6 of 89); Customer support (6 of 353); Marketing (6 of 137); Personal assistants (6 of 125).

The price mix on the shortlist is 21 open source, 8 source-available, 4 free or freemium, 3 paid, usage-based or enterprise. A startup CTO page exists if you are choosing frameworks, hosting, and security instead of the customer-facing jobs.

Who it is for

A founder, operator, or first hire who will use a ready-made agent for support, marketing, or the first app. The page's audience label is founders and small businesses. If you want to author an agent framework, you want the developer or AI-engineer role, not this one.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Each job is a short list with a link to every matching tool, so you can stop at six or keep going.
  • Starred names on the shortlist include n8n, Dify, Open WebUI, Langflow.
  • Free browser tools linked for this role (the cost calculator and the prompt builder) do not require an account.

Cons

  • The cap is six tools a section. Good tools past that only appear on the task page.
  • 0 shortlisted listings do not state a price. Support and marketing agents often do that on purpose.
  • Ready-made does not mean unattended. Tools that send email, post, or spend still need your account and your approval.

How to use it

  1. Pick the one job that is on fire: support, marketing, the first app, or the back office.
  2. Open two listings in that section. Compare the maker's price and whether it is ready to use.
  3. Check the official site for the free tier before you connect a mailbox or an ad account.
  4. If the real question is build versus buy for the whole stack, use the build-versus-buy calculator, then the startup CTO page.

FAQ

What does this page cover?

Operations, workflows, no-code apps, customer support, marketing, and personal assistants. It shows 36 tools from 1178 matches. Sections: Business operations (6 of 213); Automate workflows (6 of 511); Build apps without code (6 of 89); Customer support (6 of 353); Marketing (6 of 137); Personal assistants (6 of 125).

I am technical. Is this the wrong page?

Use it for the customer-facing and back-office jobs. Use the startup CTO page for coding agents, hosting, monitoring, and security.

Will these tools run the company while I sleep?

No. They are products you connect to your own accounts. Anything that emails, posts, or spends still waits on the permissions you grant and on the maker's product limits.
